# Interpretation detail and recovery

New desktop requests accept `detail_level`: `concise`, `standard` (the default), or `detailed`. An empty or unknown value returns a field error. For example:

```json
{
  "video_url": "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=abcdefghijk",
  "detail_level": "detailed"
}
```

All three levels read the full available transcript and require the same real-frame observation, citation, and publication checks. Detail changes information retention and explanation, with a bounded optional inspection allowance; it does not promise a fixed length, price, latency, or image count. A question can ask for more detail than its article.

GenerationSpec version 2 stores the actual policy, its content hash, detail, model revision, purpose, and source-pipeline revision in PostgreSQL. Requests and articles expose only safe detail metadata. An explicit retry inherits that specification. Historical results retain their original specification and show “原版解读”. Published articles remain immutable.

Omitted detail and explicit `standard` have the same new idempotency identity. Pre-detail URL-only and model/purpose keys can still replay their original accepted request for the historical default; those keys cannot upgrade to another detail level. Article reuse includes the complete frozen specification. Source preparation remains independent of detail and interpretation model.

## Bounded generation

The article outline records source ranges, distinct key points, and supporting `tN`/`fN` aliases. Historical timestamped outline protocols require ranges in source order. Article authoring protocols (harness 12 and later) accept a topic order: a closing chapter may revisit earlier evidence. The server derives ranges from canonical citations and checks their temporal union independently of chapter order. Ranges must stay inside the video and jointly cover it without gaps. Invalid derived ranges point to editable `evidence_ids`, never absent model-authored timestamps. This is a structural coverage check; it is not a semantic quality score. Chapter writes remain bounded and versioned. Later writing receives excerpts from accepted canonical blocks to avoid repeating earlier chapters; excerpts never replace the complete video transcript.

Authoring keeps the complete original transcript in context. Its citation catalog maps each `tN` alias to the exact zero-based `transcript_index`, instead of repeating segment IDs, origins and timestamps already present in that transcript. A cue clipped to actual media also carries its `canonical_range`; frame aliases retain actual frame IDs and times. The server continues reconstructing and validating canonical citations. Existing frozen model inputs retain their saved messages and schemas; Q&A and historical outline catalog formats are unchanged.

Protocol `interpretation-2026-10-19-v11` (harness 14) introduced a source index for each delivered image and current citation context: overlapping platform cues or nearby source text, with coarse ASR windows explicitly distinguished from sentence timing. This index helps the author check which stage a screenshot belongs to; it does not establish that narration describes the pixels. The instructions distinguish visible properties from material, identity, emotion and causal inferences, require evidence from both stages for before/after claims, and preserve uncertainty in summaries. Historical checkpoints and explicit retries keep their frozen policy and budgets.

A new private question selects its own current answer protocol even when its immutable article used an earlier policy. Question-context images enter the citation registry under their actual delivered identities and become citable only after observation. A grounded answer with a selected image must reference those pixels in its explanation or limitations; a subtitle alone cannot substitute for that reference. New article figure assembly also retains source citations for every displayed frame, without changing authored captions. These checks establish provenance, not semantic entailment: a valid citation or successful publication is not proof that every factual claim or caption is accurate.

Current new articles use `interpretation-2026-10-19-v13` (harness 16), with a publication barrier for source-grounding review of articles and historical answer contracts. Current private-question delivery is specified below. After structural validation, the selected model receives a separate review context containing the candidate, complete original transcript, actual pixels in separate chronological batches of at most three, and any read external snapshots. Writer observations, previous answers and the old article are excluded as review evidence. Review protocol v3 first checks the full candidate against the original transcript without images, preserving distinct positive/negative conditions, numbers and durations. Text defects return to repair before visual calls. Each subsequent visual call receives only canonical fields referencing its own frames, original JSON Pointers, and corresponding section headings; paths and frame citations outside that scope are rejected. A partial but accurate caption does not need to enumerate every visible detail. A malformed reviewer verdict receives its actual schema error on the one fresh format retry. New reviewer operations bind their review protocol in the content hash; existing frozen model inputs and their records remain unchanged. The reviewer returns bounded, source-linked issues at existing candidate fields; unsupported candidates remain outside the publishable operation and return to ordinary bounded repair. The reviewer cannot publish or rewrite prose. Review issues bind canonical array offsets to saved section IDs, block IDs and exact draft paths. Only chapters named by a pending review may have their display title corrected along with that chapter through save_article_sections; chapter identities, evidence, coverage and unrelated prose remain intact. Pending source corrections survive checkpoint recovery and later tool/schema errors until a fresh candidate passes review; answer corrections retain evidence/read actions and the complete submit_answer path, while draft-field patch tools are hidden. Feedback supplies actual JSON field types and bounded existing values. Review reserves up to 32,768 output tokens, bounded by the frozen model capacity, independently of the writer phase. This reservation includes provider reasoning even when the final verdict is short; the original call, time, provider and repair limits still apply. Existing harness 15 inputs retain their frozen 4,096-token reservation.

Each verdict is bound to the exact candidate, immutable source identity and frame hashes. A durable reservation allocates its normal `model:N` slot before submission, preserving frozen inputs, usage and unknown provider outcomes across worker recovery. Replaying a completed review does not send it again; changing the candidate requires a new review. Model review remains fallible, so realistic content checks and human calibration remain necessary. Harness 15 also maps an exact owned, observed frame ID used as a citation to its canonical alias; unknown identities still fail at an editable block path. Outline evidence arrays expose the same 1–24 bound already enforced by the server.

Review protocol v4 also receives the actual question when checking an answer, so a requested visual detail remains part of its scope. The question identifies what to check; it is not evidence or permission to override review rules. Hedged property claims such as a guessed manufacturing method still need support: a visible outline does not establish printing, thickness, physical composition, or a fixed expression. An unrelated disclaimer cannot support that guess. Previous review prompts and frozen calls remain intact under their own protocol identities.

Review protocol v5 keeps the text and visual responsibilities explicit after the shared grounding rules. A text-only rejection must cite supplied original text or a read external text source; a pixel-only issue receives bounded format feedback and stays out of writer repair. Text review defers visual claims to their actual image batch. When one field refers to several batches, visual review checks only the clauses about the images currently supplied, and defers the other clauses. Historical prompts, verdicts and frozen inputs remain intact.

Optional `activity_title` is action metadata. Retained candidates keep its original bytes for identity and replay, while ordinary writes and field repairs both remove it before strict body decoding. Accepted chapters remain immutable during repair. Out-of-order figure sequences identify the editable `frame_ids` field, so correcting frame order does not require replacing the caption or surrounding prose.

Tool feedback and the next-call readiness capsule report pending observations, critical questions, comparison capacity, optional inspection allowance, and remaining calls. Multiple inspection calls in one response are rejected together before creating frames or questions, with a protocol response for each call. Successful extraction still requires observation of actual pixels.

Argument errors, prerequisites, unknown tools, repeated requests, and rejected candidates contribute to durable no-progress tracking. A changed activity title, network heartbeat, or duplicate observation does not count as new evidence. Suppliers' tool-call IDs are scoped to a logical model call before dispatch so repeated provider IDs cannot replay unrelated earlier tool results.

Rejected candidates stay outside the publishable `candidate` operation. `patch_article_draft` uses version CAS and a restricted replace-only JSON Pointer subset. `read_candidate_fields` and `repair_candidate` support bounded final-answer corrections by candidate ID and exact hash. At most 16 field replacements and 64 KiB of patch data are accepted per attempt. Syntax-invalid candidates at most 64 KiB may receive one complete replacement object per authorized repair attempt; larger invalid JSON is retained and never silently truncated. Repair attempts have a separate frozen bound and consume the original run's call/time limits. Every repaired result must pass full validation before publication.

## Model budgets

The model's configured maximum output is a ceiling, not an output target. New article policies (`interpretation-2026-10-17-v7`, harness 9) cap investigation, outline/chapter composition, repair, and explicit final submission separately (8,192 / 32,768 / 16,384 / 2,048 tokens, further limited by the frozen model profile and context reservation). A ready outline receives the full composition reservation, without the historical 8,192-token pre-draft cap. Each call receives its actual output allowance and a suggested batch size. Increasing the context window alone does not remove an output cap. Q&A uses the composition allowance rather than the concise article's optional investigation allowance. Existing article runs and explicit retries retain their frozen policy.

New chapter writes accept 1–12 chapters, at most 128 KiB per batch and 512 KiB per saved draft. Chapters follow independent topics; related definitions, reasons, steps and examples belong together. The server independently validates each chapter and atomically saves the valid subset with an accepted/rejected receipt. A crash or replay returns that exact receipt; a rejected chapter cannot discard accepted siblings. The model corrects rejected IDs using the returned version, without rewriting accepted content. Draft corrections are durably bounded per draft version; planning corrections cannot exhaust a later chapter's allowance. Total call, time and no-progress limits still apply.

Once every planned chapter is saved, the server runs the same complete article/evidence/figure validator and immutable publication path automatically. It does not spend another model call just to return a version number. Failed final validation reopens only the necessary corrections and remains bounded; recovery uses the persisted draft and candidate. Optional `activity_title` metadata and explicit block/inline type enums match the new runtime contract. Plan repair advertises field replacement only, without the unsupported whole-object replacement option.

Q&A guidance introduced with harness 11 includes short canonical video references and focused answer instructions, even when reading an older article. Current new questions additionally use the source review policy described above. Each provider request requires an executable tool action; evidence tools remain available, so the model can inspect when necessary or submit directly when evidence suffices. Conceptual questions should use the supplied transcript and article first. New frames must address a concrete visual gap relevant to the question; unrelated article limitations or decorative illustrations do not justify another investigation. It cannot use an ordinary text-only stop as a successful answer. Existing initialized turn checkpoints retain their original protocol. A short direct summary is followed by supporting paragraphs, lists or a useful comparison, without repeating the conclusion in an introduction and ending. Optional `parts` give question-specific titles to existing blocks; references to missing blocks or duplicate membership are rejected. Simple answers need no artificial sections. Explicit requests for depth are not truncated to a short-answer target. Text is never interpreted as Markdown or HTML.

New articles save a concept tree in `mind_map` with the final `save_sections` action, without a separate planning or map-generation model call. Root/theme/relationship nodes can organize concepts across chapters; labels need not copy section titles. Group nodes can omit a section link, while leaves point to existing section IDs and every chapter has a linked concept. The server resolves creation keys from that same save to stable chapter IDs and validates connectedness, cycles, depth, node count and navigation targets. Subsequent batches and exact replay preserve the map when omitted; a map-only save can complete an already saved body. Invalid maps retain accepted prose and return actionable feedback, but cannot produce a completed article. Deleting linked chapters requires an updated map in the same atomic save. Already admitted older runs retain their original contract, and historical published maps remain immutable. A side outline is generated directly from published chapters for navigation.

Optional article investigation enters wrap-up after 4 / 6 / 10 logical calls or 120 / 240 / 480 seconds of cumulative model-response time for concise / standard / detailed, or when at most eight calls remain. These are prompts to prioritize writing and limitations, not whole-task deadlines or permission to bypass outstanding critical evidence and final validation. Phase-specific tool schemas reduce irrelevant actions; a rejected draft retains the exact read and repair tools needed to address its validation errors.

Model profiles define `input_chars_limit` and `context_tokens` independently of output. Zero context tokens means unknown capacity. For imported combined-character profiles, an unset input limit preserves their previous effective allowance, `max_input_chars - 4 * max_output_tokens`; the selected phase's output no longer repeatedly consumes that envelope. Legacy checkpoints keep their previous path.

Text and image token reserves are explicitly heuristic and model dependent. Images still have the existing 12-frame/16 MiB working-set limits. A configured shared context limit checks text estimate + image reserve + selected output + safety reserve. This is not a provider tokenizer or billing measurement. Unreported provider input/output/cache usage remains unknown.

New bounded-source executions use a separate output reservation for source navigation cards and keep article output bounded per chapter batch. A longer transcript does not reserve a response proportional to the entire original source. Saved chapters are assembled by the server. Large-source review receives the unit's complete cited originals and chronological neighbors, the full navigation ledger, actual required pixels and host-owned scope facts. Derived notes cannot prove a claim; a required evidence set that cannot fit fails explicitly instead of being silently trimmed and approved. Historical frozen execution contracts retain their previous input and output rules.

Before any physical model submission, the logical input, budget, tool schemas, and durable image references are saved. Worker takeover reuses that input. PostgreSQL JSON object key order cannot change its canonical argument hash. Incompatible old frozen tools fail explicitly instead of silently changing an already accepted operation.

## Subtitle adoption and diagnostics

Supported subtitle formats are selected before language preference can send an unsupported track to ASR. Extraction uses the metadata that supplied the track, tries at most three candidates, and places subtitle files in their own bounded directory. This avoids a second metadata selection and prevents a retained video from consuming a subtitle-only quota. Bilibili subtitle availability and manual/automatic/unknown kind are recorded separately.

Selection, download, parse outcome, cue coverage, fallback reason, and adopted text origin are durable source diagnostics. Coverage uses the existing cue-duration union thresholds: 50% usable and 85% complete. It does not infer speech or silence. ASR retains 60-second windows and coarse timing where the provider supplies no segments. Two workers use two globally fenced ASR lanes; completed windows commit independently, while transcript ordering and progress follow contiguous source windows. Recovery reuses completed windows, and cancellation joins workers before leaving. A rejected subtitle's language is not reported as an ASR language detection.

For YouTube without usable subtitles, bounded video and audio downloads can overlap in separate leased lanes. The combined download limit and full video verification still apply; prefetched audio is canceled when unnecessary. Source preparation, subtitles, video download, audio download, media verification and transcription have separately retained activity steps and elapsed times. New requests freeze `subtitle-adoption-parallel-v3` as their source-pipeline revision, keeping new metadata/window checkpoints separate from older cached preparation contracts. Redis coordinates short-lived admission; PostgreSQL remains the authority for checkpoints, accepted drafts, observations and publication. Adding Redis copies of those records would not remove provider response latency.

Extractor metadata has a separate 32 MiB stdout allowance because many caption languages can produce more than 10 MiB of signed format/track URLs. Ordinary media-tool stdout remains bounded at 4 MiB. Any overflow fails explicitly; a truncated buffer is never parsed or published as a complete source. Metadata decode diagnostics retain size/limit and validity, without exposing signed URLs or raw extractor output.

Title and duration can appear before download and source publication. Failures include a safe diagnostic ID; HTTP responses include a generated `X-Request-ID`, also used in problem responses and linked to request/turn creation.

Owner run diagnostics include bounded `tool_rejections`: tool/call identity, consecutive no-progress count, and validation categories with field paths. Missing retained fields are identified as `repair_field_missing`. This projection excludes raw rejection prose, expected/actual candidate values, prompts and signed URLs. Unknown rejection text is represented by a generic category. The original private execution ledger remains available for authorized deeper investigation.

String-encoded tool errors and nested chapter validation issues use the same safe projection. Only known error envelopes are traversed, to a bounded depth and at most 64 issue entries per rejection; raw chapter or candidate contents are not returned.

When short execution feedback clips a long validation report, diagnostics uses the complete retained issue bound to the same run and tool call. Candidate arguments and private expected/actual values remain outside the diagnostic response.


## Private question delivery (V6)

All newly admitted private questions use `qa-article-reading-2026-10-30-v7`, independently of the immutable article writer. V6 retains the delivery behavior below; V7 also provides explicit host-owned comparison scope to the reviewer and readers. Local samples cannot support a simultaneous whole-video uniqueness claim, even when the author adds a parenthetical qualification. Attributed speaker claims still require their actual originals. Frozen older question checkpoints retain their original schemas and publication policy. The current round and stage timing policy remains unchanged.

The author classifies actual delivered pixels against the current question. `refine_video_frames` accepts one or two owned, delivered low-information frames and a short visible deficiency. It batches two real temporal samples per parent, initially within a narrow neighborhood and then a wider one, clamped to source duration. At most two refinement batches (eight additional candidates) are admitted per turn. Plans, parent inspection identities, reason and budget are saved before extraction; replay reuses their media operations and cannot create new credit. Real decoded frame timestamps and source identities remain canonical. No separate model runs classify each frame. White or black backgrounds alone are not rejected, and no image-quality score is invented. Semantic selection still depends on the author observing the returned pixels.

Only frames actually cited by an answer require observations; unrelated fetched candidates do not block submission or the next targeted inspection. The citation catalog still admits frames only after observation. The author should stop when a useful image suffices and state remaining gaps, rather than fill a picture quota or claim exhaustive video coverage from local samples.

After structural/source validation, the server saves an owner-scoped provisional interpretation in a `question_preview` operation and emits a snapshot event before independent review. It does not set the committed answer. The review receives the existing separate original-text/pixel context and durable unit identities. A fully supported candidate is delivered with `review=passed`; mixed results retain only supported semantic blocks in the confirmed body and move other claims and reasons into explicit uncertainties. If no usable verdict is available, a limited reference interpretation retains at most three cited blocks, removes known rejected claims, and says `review=unavailable`. Unassessed does not mean contradicted or approved. Author limitations remain visible. This is a qualified answer, not a source-quality guarantee or a replacement for human checks.

A server-created `question_delivery` receipt binds the exact final answer, review status and current source identity. Model-authored metadata cannot grant approval; changing content, evidence status or source bytes invalidates the receipt. Cancellation, lost execution ownership, storage errors and the expired parent round do not authorize fallback publication. A provisional interpretation may remain visible with its interrupted status. An individual review-call deadline or unavailable/empty reviewer result can produce labeled limited delivery while the parent round is still live. Ordinary bounded transport retries remain distinct from a new author correction loop and are counted as actual sends.


## Prepared video questions

New article-loop admissions freeze `article_questions_policy=article-questions-2026-10-30-v1`. After the immutable article is published, an independent article-level record queues question preparation before optional review on the existing background queue. It receives the article title and bounded excerpts across chapters, not private chat history or image pixels. The request asks for 3–4 distinct, concise questions with optional canonical section IDs; no question is answered at this stage. Input and output are bounded, output reservation is at most 2,048 tokens, and the request has a 90-second deadline. These are requested limits rather than a guarantee about provider billing reports.

PostgreSQL holds admission, epoch, input hash, the one-submission fence, result, expiry and usage reporting. Queue redelivery and page reads cannot grant another physical submission. Errors and interrupted/expired preparation leave an incomplete artifact; the article remains readable and its content hash unchanged. Legacy admissions and reused old articles receive no automatic backfill. This auxiliary request is separate from article-writing and private-Q&A call ledgers, like the existing optional article review; it is not charged again for each new conversation. The questions are model suggestions, not independently verified answers.
